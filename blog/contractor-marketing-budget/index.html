How much should a contractor spend on marketing?

By Malakai Bubar, Local Forge Media · Updated November 16, 2026

There is no single right percentage. The honest answer depends on your margins, how many jobs your crew can handle, and how fast you want to grow. What you can do is set a budget with a method, instead of guessing.

Here is the approach we'd use.

Start from the jobs you want, not a percentage

Work backward. Decide how many extra booked jobs you can handle per month, estimate your average profit per job, and figure out roughly what you could pay to win one. That gives you a ceiling that makes sense for your business.

Know your numbers first

You need three: your average job value, your close rate on estimates, and how many leads it takes to book a job. Even rough versions help. Without them, any budget is a guess.

Separate fees from ad spend

Marketing costs usually have two parts: what you pay a person or agency to run things, and what you pay platforms like Meta or Google for ads. Ad spend needs enough budget to give the ads room to learn, so a very small test often tells you little.

Count the free and cheap fixes

A complete Google Business Profile, a fast website with a clear way to book, reviews and fast follow-up cost little and improve everything that comes after. Do these before raising ad spend.

Commit for long enough to learn

Plan on at least three months before judging results. Ads need time to settle, and local SEO builds more slowly. Judging after two weeks usually leads to quitting right before it works, or doubling down on something that isn't.

Raise spend only where jobs come from

Track cost per booked job by channel. Put more into what books jobs, cut what doesn't, and keep spend in line with what your crew can actually complete.

Common questions

What percentage of revenue should a contractor spend on marketing?

There's no universal number. Set it from the jobs you want, your margins and your capacity, then adjust using cost per booked job.

How long before contractor marketing pays off?

Ads can bring leads within days, but give any plan at least three months to judge. Local SEO and reviews build over longer periods. Nobody can honestly guarantee results.

Should I spend on ads or on my website first?

Fix the basics first: your Google Business Profile, a site that makes booking easy, and fast follow-up. Ads work better on top of that foundation.
