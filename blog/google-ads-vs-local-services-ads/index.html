Google Ads vs. Local Services Ads for contractors

By Malakai Bubar, Local Forge Media · Updated November 18, 2026

Google Ads charges you for clicks on your ads. Local Services Ads (LSAs) charge you for valid leads, such as calls and messages, and only show to people searching for a local service. For many contractors the right answer is both, used for different jobs. This guide explains how each works, what published cost numbers do and don't tell you, and what's changing in 2026.

How they work

TargetingKeywords you choose, plus locations and schedulesService categories and areas; Google matches the searches
Where it showsSearch results and other Google placementsTop of local search results

Based on Google's help pages for Local Services Ads billing and screening.

What Google says about LSA billing

  • You pay for valid leads, and prices vary by location, job type, lead type and bidding mode.
  • Valid leads include calls, missed calls over 20 seconds during business hours, texts and email, voicemails and booking requests.
  • Charged leads are reviewed over about 30 days and may be credited automatically if they're low quality, duplicates or invalid. You can also report a lead with the feedback survey.

Eligibility and screening

LSAs require a verified, public Google Business Profile, and screening typically takes around three to four weeks. Most home service categories need business registration, general liability insurance and the right state or local licenses. Urgent categories such as electrician, HVAC, plumbing, garage door and locksmith get extra checks, including background checks on the people doing the work. Start this early if you want to run LSAs.

What do the leads actually cost?

Published numbers conflict, and it's worth knowing why. Cost per lead differs by trade, city, season, and how each study defines a "lead." A few reference points, with caveats:

  • LocaliQ publishes median Google search cost per lead for home services, from roughly $39 (pest control) and $49 (plumbing) up to about $101 for roofing and gutters. Its page states the underlying data period is May 2020 to June 2021, so treat these as a floor, not today's price. (LocaliQ)
  • Agency benchmark summaries of a SearchLight Digital study (February 2026, 888 contractors) report LSA leads at around $53 versus roughly $104 for search ads. We could not open the original report, so treat it as directional.
  • Other sources put roofing at $200 or more per lead. That's why you'll see "$50" and "$250" quoted for the same ads.

The honest takeaway: expect anywhere from about $40 to $230 or more per lead depending on the trade and market, and get your own numbers from a small test.

Measure cost per booked job, not cost per lead

A cheap lead that never answers the phone costs more than an expensive one that books. Track this chain: ad spend, leads, qualified leads, estimates, booked jobs. Then divide spend by booked jobs.

Example (illustrative numbers): $2,000 in spend produces 40 leads ($50 each). If 10 are real, qualified inquiries and 4 become booked jobs, your cost per booked job is $500. Another campaign at $90 per lead might produce 25 leads, 15 qualified and 7 booked jobs, which is about $257 per booked job. The "expensive" leads won.

A "valid lead" to Google just means a relevant inquiry. It doesn't mean the person is in your service area, ready to buy, or the decision-maker. That's the gap lead qualification is meant to close; see our guide on how an AI receptionist qualifies and books leads.

What's changing in 2026

According to Search Engine Journal (July 20, 2026), Google began moving Local Services Ads into Google Ads from August 2026, starting with a small group of US advertisers and expanding through the year. Reported changes include keywordless, pay-per-lead Performance Max-style campaigns that show on Search and Maps, a daily average budget replacing the weekly budget, and the removal of manual bidding. Billing is reported to stay per valid lead. Details are still being clarified, and some vendor blogs claim further billing changes for missed calls and texts, so check your own account for what applies to you. (Search Engine Journal)

Which should you use?

  • Emergency and repair work (plumbing, HVAC repair, electrical, locksmith): LSAs are often a strong fit because people want a call right now.
  • Planned and higher-ticket work (roof replacement, remodeling, system replacement): search ads with a dedicated landing page give you more control over who clicks and what they see.
  • Meta ads (Facebook and Instagram) work well for planned jobs and maintenance plans, where you reach homeowners before they start searching.
  • All of them depend on answering calls fast. A missed call in a paid campaign is wasted money.

Before you spend

  • Make sure your Google Business Profile is complete and verified.
  • Decide who answers calls and how fast.
  • Set a test budget you can sustain for at least three months.
  • Track calls and booked jobs by source from day one.

Common questions

Are Local Services Ads better than Google Ads for contractors?

Neither is better in every case. LSAs suit urgent, high-intent calls and charge per valid lead. Search ads give more control and suit planned, higher-ticket work. Many contractors use both.

How much do Local Services Ads cost?

Google says prices vary by location, job type and lead type. Published benchmarks range widely, roughly $40 to $230 or more per lead depending on the trade and source, so run a small test in your market.

Can I get a refund for bad Local Services leads?

Google reviews charged leads over about 30 days and may credit low-quality, duplicate or invalid ones automatically. You can also submit feedback on a lead. Check Google's current help page for the exact rules.

Do I need Local Services Ads to be on Google's map results?

No. Organic map results come from your Google Business Profile, not from ads. See our guide to local SEO for contractors.
