Best lead generation companies for contractors: how to choose

By Malakai Bubar, Local Forge Media · Updated November 18, 2026

The best lead generation company for a contractor is the one that brings you qualified, booked jobs at a cost per booked job you can afford, while you keep ownership of your accounts and data. There isn't one winner for every trade and city, so instead of a ranked list we'll show you the main types of provider, what each is good and bad at, and the questions that separate honest vendors from the rest.

Judge them by cost per booked job, not cost per lead

A lead is only a name and a phone number. What you care about is how much you spend for each job you actually book. Ask every vendor to explain the chain: spend, leads, qualified leads, estimates, booked jobs. Then divide your total spend by booked jobs. A provider with expensive leads can still be cheaper per job if those leads are qualified and exclusive to you. We walk through an example in our guide to Google Ads vs. Local Services Ads.

Questions to ask any lead generation company

  1. Are the leads exclusive to me, or sold to several contractors?
  2. How do you define a lead? A form fill, a call, or a screened, booked appointment?
  3. What happens before a lead reaches me? Is anyone, or any software, checking service area, job type, timeline and budget? (Here's the qualification checklist we use.)
  4. Who owns the ad accounts, the website, the phone numbers and the data? You should, and you should be able to take them with you.
  5. How do you track results? Call tracking, form tracking and booked jobs by source, shown in plain-language reports.
  6. What's the contract? Length, notice to cancel, and what happens to your accounts when you leave.
  7. What does it cost in total? Retainer, ad spend, setup fees and any per-lead charges.
  8. Can I talk to a current client? Ask for a reference in a similar trade and market.
  9. What won't you promise? A good vendor will tell you what they can't control.

Red flags

  • Guaranteed lead counts or guaranteed rankings. Nobody controls how many people search, call or book. We don't guarantee results either.
  • "Custom pricing" with no ranges and no scope in writing.
  • The vendor holds your ad accounts or website. If you can't log in as the owner, you don't own it.
  • No tracking or vague reports, such as screenshots of impressions with no calls or booked jobs.
  • Long contracts with penalties and no way to review performance.
  • Self-ranked "best of" lists. Many "best lead generation companies" pages are written by the companies they rank, so check who published it.

A simple way to decide

  1. Pick a budget you can sustain for at least three months.
  2. Start with the cheapest way to test demand, such as Local Services Ads or a small search campaign, and track booked jobs by source.
  3. Add an owned channel (local SEO, a good website and a booking assistant) so you're not renting every lead.
  4. Keep the provider that lowers your cost per booked job, and drop the rest.

Common questions

What is the best lead generation company for contractors?

There's no single best. The right choice depends on your trade, market, budget and how much you want to own. Judge providers by cost per booked job, lead exclusivity, tracking and whether you keep ownership of your accounts and data.

Are lead marketplaces worth it for contractors?

They can get you leads quickly, but leads may be shared with other contractors and you don't own the data. Track your cost per booked job against other channels before relying on them.

How much do contractor leads cost?

It varies widely by trade and market. Published benchmarks range from roughly $40 to $230 or more per lead, with roofing at the high end. See our guide to Google Ads vs. Local Services Ads for sources and caveats.

Should I sign a long contract with a lead generation company?

Be cautious. Prefer clear scope in writing, a short minimum term to test results, and full ownership of your accounts and data so you can leave if it isn't working.
