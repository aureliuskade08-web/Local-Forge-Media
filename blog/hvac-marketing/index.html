HVAC marketing: 8 ways to fill your schedule

By Malakai Bubar, Local Forge Media · Updated November 16, 2026

Most HVAC companies don't have a lead problem in July or January. They have a lead problem in the shoulder seasons, and a conversion problem all year: calls that go to voicemail, websites that don't make it obvious how to book, and ads that bring in price shoppers.

This is the order we'd work through it for an HVAC contractor. It starts with the free fixes and gets to paid ads last, because ads only work well on top of a solid foundation.

1. Fix your Google Business Profile first

For most HVAC searches, the map results at the top of Google decide who gets the call. Make sure your profile has the right primary category (HVAC contractor), your real hours, every service you offer listed by name, your service area, and recent photos of your trucks and crew. Add your website and booking link. Then keep it current: a profile with no new photos or reviews in a year looks abandoned.

2. Build a website that books jobs

A homeowner with a dead AC is not browsing. Your site needs a tap-to-call number at the top, a clear way to request service, and a separate page for each thing you sell: AC repair, furnace repair, system replacement, maintenance plans. Each page should say what you do, which areas you cover, what it typically involves and how to book. If your site is slow on a phone or hides the phone number, you're paying for traffic you can't convert. This is what a website redesign built for home service contractors fixes.

3. Add local SEO pages for the areas you serve

Google needs a reason to show you in each town. Write one useful page per major area you serve, with details that are actually true: the neighborhoods, common system types, how quickly you can get there. Skip thin pages that only swap the city name. A few genuine pages beat thirty copies.

4. Use search ads for urgent work and Meta ads for planned work

Emergency repairs are a search game: someone types a problem and wants a tech today. Planned work is different. Furnace tune-ups, system replacements and maintenance plans can be sold before the homeowner is shopping, and Facebook and Instagram ads are good at reaching homeowners in your service area with a specific offer. Keep each ad to one offer and one audience, and send people to a page built for that offer rather than your homepage.

5. Plan around the seasons

Run tune-up offers a few weeks ahead of the first hot and cold weeks, not during them. Run replacement offers when systems are old and homeowners are thinking about energy bills. A simple calendar of what you promote each month keeps your marketing from reacting to the weather instead of getting ahead of it.

6. Ask for reviews on every job

Reviews drive both rankings and trust. Send the request by text the same day the job is done, with a direct link. Reply to every review, including the bad ones, in a calm and specific way. Prospects read how you respond.

7. Answer every call, including after hours

Speed to lead is where a lot of HVAC revenue leaks out. If nobody answers, the homeowner calls the next company on the list. A receptionist or an AI receptionist and chatbot on your website can answer common questions, collect the problem and address, and book the visit on your calendar when your office is closed.

8. Track booked jobs, not clicks

Judge marketing by cost per booked job and revenue per job type. Use call tracking and a simple sheet that records where each job came from. Clicks and impressions tell you whether ads are being seen, not whether they're working.

Common questions

How much should an HVAC company spend on marketing?

There's no single right number. It depends on your margins, capacity and how many booked jobs you need. Start with a budget you can sustain for at least three months, track cost per booked job, and increase spend only on what's producing jobs.

What is the fastest way for an HVAC company to get more leads?

Fixing your Google Business Profile and making sure every call gets answered are usually the fastest wins. Paid ads can bring leads quickly, but they work best once those basics are in place.

Do HVAC companies need Facebook ads?

Not all do, but they're useful for planned work like tune-ups, maintenance plans and replacements, where you can reach homeowners before they start shopping.
