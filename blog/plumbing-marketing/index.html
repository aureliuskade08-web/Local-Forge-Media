Plumbing marketing: what actually brings in calls

By Malakai Bubar, Local Forge Media · Updated November 16, 2026

Plumbing work comes in two kinds. Emergencies, like a burst pipe or a backed-up drain, where the customer picks whoever answers first. And planned jobs, like water heater replacements, repipes and remodel work, where they compare options. Good plumbing marketing treats those two differently.

Here is what we'd put in place for a plumbing company, in order of how quickly it tends to pay off.

Be the first answer for emergencies

People with an emergency search on their phone and call from the map results or the first ads. That means your Google Business Profile needs to be complete and accurate, with your real hours (and 24/7 availability if you actually offer it), a phone number that is answered, and a clear service area. If you can't answer every call, route missed calls to a receptionist or an AI receptionist and chatbot that captures the problem and address and gets you the details right away.

Give every service its own page

Drain cleaning, water heaters, leak repair, sewer line, repiping and fixture installs are different searches with different customers. A page for each one, with what you do, what it generally involves, the areas you cover and a clear call to action, gives Google something to rank and gives the customer a reason to trust you. This is the kind of structure we build into a website redesign for home service contractors.

Use search ads where intent is highest

Search ads on urgent terms reach people who need a plumber now. They are also where costs can climb, so set a budget cap, exclude irrelevant searches (jobs, DIY, wholesale) and send people to a page that matches their search. Review the search terms report weekly.

Use Meta ads for planned work and maintenance plans

Water heater replacement, whole-home filtration, repipes and maintenance memberships can be marketed on Facebook and Instagram to homeowners in your area. Lead with one clear offer, show your real team, and use a short form or a booking link. Follow up quickly, because planned-work leads cool off fast.

Collect reviews and show them

Ask every satisfied customer for a Google review right after the job. Put the best ones on your service pages. Specific reviews that mention the problem and the town help both trust and local rankings.

Follow up on every estimate

Many estimates go quiet. A simple text and email follow-up a day or two later, and again a week later, recovers jobs that would otherwise go to a competitor. Track every estimate you send and its outcome.

Measure what matters

Track calls, booked jobs and revenue by source. If a channel brings calls but not jobs, change the offer or the page, not just the budget.

Common questions

What is the best marketing for plumbers?

A complete Google Business Profile, a fast website with a page for each service, reviews, and a way to answer or capture every call are the core. Paid search and Meta ads then add volume on top.

Should plumbers use Meta ads or Google ads?

Both can work for different jobs. Google ads suit urgent searches. Meta ads suit planned work like water heater replacements and maintenance plans.

How do plumbers get more calls without paying for leads?

Improve your Google Business Profile, build service pages, collect reviews consistently and make sure every call is answered. These compound over time.
